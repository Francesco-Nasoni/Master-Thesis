\section{ODT loading optimization}
The optimal parameters obtained for the molasses were used for the optimization of the ODT loading. This optimization was mainly focussed on the compensation magnetic field. The idea is to load the MOT and before switching off the magnetic field, exploit the compensation magnetic field to shift the MOT position. Indeed, the MOT is formed where the magnetic field is approximately zero, this by adjusting the compensation coil currents, it is possible to shift the zero and move the MOT. This makes the MOT loose atom since it is moved away from the optimal position of the intersection of the beams, but there is a trade-off with the better intersection with the ODT beam, that increase the number of captured atoms. As long as this shift is small the molasses optimal parameters found in the previous sections of this chapter should nullify the residual magnetic field well, and they do not need a second revision.

In particular the compensation coil currents are adjusted to the loading values after load with 200~ms ramp in 20 steps. The time and the ramp are needed to make the translation smooth, in order to minimize the loosing of atom in the process.

\subsubsection{Preliminary measurements}
The first measurements were taken using the FLIR camera and by employing the optimal parameters from the molasses temperature optimization.

Unfortunately using the analysis described in Section \ref{subsec:odt_loading_analysis_method}, both in the two-dimensional and flattened one-dimensional images there was any ODT signal visible.
This was caused by two factors, the difference of the image with the ODT on and off have a residual background large compared with the expected signal of the ODT, therefore the signal is submerged in the noise, second the ODT beam radius is small compared with the cloud radius, therefore the ODT should be visible just in on few number of pixel.
